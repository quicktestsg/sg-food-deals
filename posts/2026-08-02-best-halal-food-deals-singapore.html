Best Halal Food Deals in Singapore 2026 — Cheap Halal Eats Under $15

Finding halal food in Singapore that doesn't cost a fortune is easier than you think. From $3.50 nasi padang at hawker centres to halal-certified buffets, Muslim-friendly cafes in Kampong Glam, and Ramadan specials in Geylang Serai — this is your complete guide to cheap halal eats under $15.

Singapore is one of the most Muslim-friendly food cities on Earth. Nearly 15% of the population is Muslim, halal certification is widespread and rigorous, and you can find everything from $3 hawker meals to multi-course halal buffets without leaving the MRT network.

Level 1: Halal Hawker Stalls (S$3.50-8)

The backbone of cheap halal eating. Many hawker centres have dedicated halal stalls — usually marked with a red signboard or an official MUIS halal logo — serving Malay, Indian Muslim, and Middle Eastern food at the same low prices as the rest of the centre.

Where to hunt:

  • Geylang Serai Market: The spiritual home of Malay food in Singapore. Upstairs you'll find legendary nasi padang, biryani, and kueh stalls.
  • Tampines Round Market & Food Centre: A halal-stall goldmine in the East. Famous for cheap roti prata, mee siam, and sup kambing at S$3-5 a bowl.
  • Bedok Interchange Hawker Centre: Reliable halal Chinese Muslim stalls serving economical fried rice, noodles, and dumplings at S$4-6.
  • Maxwell Food Centre & Chinatown Complex: Yes, even in the heart of Chinese Singapore — look for the Muslim-owned stalls tucked among the rest.
💡 Hawker Hack

Don't see a halal logo? Ask the stall owner directly — many are Muslim-owned but haven't gone through MUIS certification due to cost. For guaranteed certified stalls, use the Verify Halal app or search MUIS's official directory before you go.

Level 2: Muslim-Friendly Chain Restaurants (S$5-12)

This is where the deals scene really opens up. Singapore's big chains run halal-certified outlets with the same rotating promos:

  • McDonald's Singapore: Fully halal-certified across all outlets — yes, the whole country. Watch for McSpicy 2-for-S$8 promos and lunch sets from S$5.95.
  • KFC Singapore: Also fully halal. The S$5 two-piece deals and weekday set meals are among the cheapest full meals in any mall.
  • Burger King & Texas Chicken: Both halal-certified. Texas Chicken's Mon/Tue 4-piece S$9.90 deal is legendary value.
  • Secret Recipe: Halal-certified and surprisingly affordable — whole cakes under S$50, lunch sets with main plus cake for S$12-14.
  • Encik Tan: Halal Chinese food chain. Economic rice, noodles, and set meals at S$5-8 — a halal alternative to the classic chap cheng stall.

Level 3: Hip Halal Cafes (S$8-15)

The halal cafe scene in Singapore has exploded — and Kampong Glam is ground zero. Haji Lane and Arab Street are packed with Muslim-friendly cafes serving specialty coffee, fusion burgers, and Insta-worthy brunch plates.

  • Coffee Bean & Tea Leaf: Halal-certified nationwide. App promos drop drinks to S$4-5 and breakfast sets to S$8-10.
  • Fika Swedish Cafe: A Beach Road staple, fully halal. Waffles, pastries, and coffee combos at S$10-14.
  • Going Om: Hip Haji Lane spot with vegan-friendly, halal-conscious fare. Smoothie bowls and loaded toasts in the S$12-15 range.
  • Chong Jen Ng Cafe: Halal Chinese-Muslim fusion. Noodle bowls and set lunches at S$9-12.
💸 Cafe Deal Hack

Many Kampong Glam cafes run weekday lunch sets (11:30am-2:30pm) that knock 25-40% off à la carte prices. Burpple Beyond also lists several halal-certified cafes with 1-for-1 main courses.

Level 4: Halal Buffet Deals (Under S$15 at Lunch)

Halal buffets in Singapore used to be a S$50+ affair. Not anymore. A new wave of halal-certified buffets has pushed lunch prices into the S$12-20 range, and the lower end is genuinely under S$15 if you hit weekday lunch:

  • Halal hotel buffets (Crowne Plaza, Shangri-La Rasa Sentosa, Grand Copthorne): Run certified halal buffets with weekday lunch tiers from S$25-35. Watch for off-peak promos and credit card 1-for-1 deals.
  • Todos Amigos: Halal international buffet. Weekday lunch around S$18-25 with seasonal flash deals that dip below S$15.
  • Suki-Ya & Pepper Lunch: Halal-certified steamboat and teppan options with set lunches at S$12-15.

Level 5: Ramadan & Hari Raya Specials

If you're in Singapore during Ramadan, the food scene transforms completely — and the deals scene goes into overdrive. Ramadan bazaars pop up across the island, the biggest at Geylang Serai, Kampong Glam, and Tampines.

  • Geylang Serai Ramadan Bazaar: The largest in Singapore. Murtabak, vadai, kebabs, ayam panggang, and viral TikTok foods — most items S$3-8. Go after 4pm, bring cash, expect crowds.
  • Kampong Glam Bazaar: Smaller but trendier. Hipster-fusion takes on traditional Malay and Arab food — salted egg ayam, rendang burgers, pistachio kunafa. Prices S$5-12.
  • Iftar buffet deals: During Ramadan, many halal restaurants run iftar set meals at promotional prices — S$15-25 multi-course spreads.
  • Hari Raya Puasa (Eid al-Fitr): Post-Ramadan brings open house buffets, kuih bundles, and family set menus.
🌙 Ramadan Timing Tip

Ramadan bazaars come alive from about 4pm until maghrib (around 7:10pm). Arrive at 4:30pm for the best selection and shortest queues — by 6pm the viral stalls have hour-long lines.

How to Verify Halal Certification (Do This First)

Singapore's halal certification is run by MUIS (the Islamic Religious Council of Singapore), and it's considered one of the most rigorous in the world. Every certified establishment displays a halal certificate — but certificates expire, so verify before you commit.

  • Look for the official MUIS halal logo at the entrance, counter, or menu.
  • Check the certificate's expiry date — printed on the certificate itself.
  • Search the official MUIS Halal Directory at halal.muis.gov.sg — covers every certified outlet in Singapore.
  • Use apps: The Verify Halal app (official MUIS) lets you scan or search on the go.
⚠️ A Note on "Muslim-Owned"

Muslim-owned does not automatically mean halal-certified. Many excellent Muslim-owned eateries operate without formal certification — but if you require formal certification, always verify through MUIS.
